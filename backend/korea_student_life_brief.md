# Korean University Student Life — Advisor Briefing (for student consultation)

*Prepared 2026-08-27 · All prices in USD at 1,400 KRW = $1 (round up to nearest $100), consistent with bot output.*

---

## 1. Monthly living costs (official government figures — Study in Korea)

| Category | KRW / month | USD / month |
|---|---|---|
| **Housing** (dorm/rent) | 500,000–700,000 | $360–$500 |
| **Food** | 200,000–300,000 | $150–$220 |
| **Transportation** | 50,000–100,000 | $40–$80 |
| **Other** (phone, internet, insurance) | 100,000–200,000 | $80–$150 |
| **TOTAL** | **750,000–1,000,000** | **$540–$720** |

💡 Rule of thumb to tell students: **budget about $600–$750/month living costs in Seoul** on top of tuition. Outside Seoul is ~15–25% cheaper.

Source: studyinkorea.go.kr/eng/life/livingExpense.do (official Korean government site).

## 2. Housing options

| Option | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **University dormitory (기숙사)** | ~$200–$1,200 / semester depending on room | Cheapest & safest; priority for freshmen & international students; meals often included |
| **One-room / goshiwon (고시원)** | ~$280–$430 / month | Small private room, no deposit needed |
| **Share house / co-living** | ~$290–$500 / month | Common for internationals |
| **Monthly rent (월세) apartment** | ~$400–$700 / month + deposit | Usually needs ₩5–10M deposit |

**Example — Hanyang Univ dorm (2026-1, official):**
- 4-person room: ₩754,000/semester ≈ **$600**
- 2-person room (private bath): ₩1,639,000/semester ≈ **$1,200**
- 1-person room (private bath): ₩2,297,000/semester ≈ **$1,700**

**Yonsei dorm example:** 무악 double room ₩1,112,000 ≈ **$800**/semester.

## 3. Food costs

- University cafeteria (학생식당): **₩3,000–5,000** ≈ **$3–4** per meal (cheapest option)
- Regular restaurant meal (Seoul): ~₩11,000 ≈ **$8**
- McDonald's set: ~₩8,000 ≈ **$6**
- Groceries: milk ₩2,920, eggs(12) ₩4,460, rice(1kg) ₩4,640, bananas(1kg) ₩4,800

💡 Cafeteria 2x/day + cook at home = keep food under **$150/month**.

## 4. Transportation

- Bus/subway single trip (Seoul): **₩1,500** ≈ **$1**
- Monthly transit pass: **₩61,300** ≈ **$50**
- Taxi base fare: ₩4,800
- Student transport cards (알뜰교통카드) give cashback on public transit
- Seoul subway/bus transfer is free within 30 min → very affordable city

---

## 5. National Health Insurance (mandatory for international students)

- **2026 monthly premium: ₩79,320 ≈ $60** (was ₩76,390 in 2025)
- Automatic for degree students staying 6+ months (D-2)
- Covers ~40–70% of medical costs (same as Korean citizens)
- Must keep paying while in Korea; exempt if absent >1 month

## 6. Part-time work (아르바이트) rules for D-2 students

**⚠️ MUST get "time-part-time work permit" (시간제 취업허가) BEFORE working** — working without it = illegal, can lose visa.

**Allowed hours (per week):**
| Student | TOPIK met | TOPIK not met |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor (학사) | **25h weekday** + unlimited weekends/vacation | 10h weekday |
| Master's/PhD | **30h weekday** + unlimited weekends/vacation | 15h weekday |

- Conditions: last semester GPA ≥ C, Korean ability (TOPIK 3+ for lower years, TOPIK 4+ for 3rd-4th yr)
- Allowed jobs: translation, restaurant helper, office assistant, tourism guide, duty-free helper
- **Restricted**: delivery riders, couriers, insurance agents, manufacturing (unless TOPIK 4+), construction
- Max 2 jobs at once; must work within 60–90 min of campus/residence during semester
- Change employer = re-apply / report within 15 days

💡 Realistic student income: part-time café/restaurant pays ~₩10,000–12,000/hour ≈ **$7–9/hr**. 25h/wk ≈ **$700–900/month** — can cover most living costs.

## 7. Student life & culture

- **Clubs (동아리)**: every campus has hundreds — sports, music, K-culture, volunteering, international clubs. Best way to make Korean friends.
- **MT (Membership Training)**: group trip at semester start — hiking/pension overnight with classmates. Great bonding, optional but recommended.
- **University festivals (축제)**: spring (May–June) & fall (Sep–Oct). Famous K-pop artists perform, student-run food/drink booths, games, night markets on campus. Free concerts.
- **대동제 (festival week)**: big annual event — must-experience.
- **Orientation (오리엔테이션)**: international student orientation at semester start — attend for dorm, visa, phone/bank setup info.
- **Buddy programs**: most unis pair internationals with Korean buddies.
- **Korean language**: daily life requires basic Korean; cafeterias, dorms, shops mostly Korean-only signage.

---

## 8. Key setup steps for new students (first 2 weeks)

1. Apply for **Alien Registration Card (외국인등록증)** within 90 days of arrival
2. Open a **bank account** (required for phone, housing, salary)
3. Get a **Korean SIM / phone plan**
4. Register for **National Health Insurance**
5. Attend **international student orientation**
6. Apply for **dorm** early (limited spots — deadline often before arrival)

## 10. D-2 vs D-4 visa — why D-2 is better (if possible)

### What each one is
| | **D-2 유학 (Student)** | **D-4 일반연수 (General Training)** |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | **Degree program** (bachelor/master's/PhD) | **Language course** (어학연수) or short training |
| Issued for | Full degree duration (e.g. 4 years BA) | Usually **6 months at a time**, max ~2 years |
| What it lets you study | University degree | Korean language only (no degree) |

### 🚨 #1 reason: Part-time work (아르바이트)
- **D-2 (degree)**: Eligible for **시간제 취업허가 (part-time work permit)** → up to **25h/week** (BA, with TOPIK) or **30h/week** (grad), unlimited weekends/vacations
- **D-4-1 (language)**: Part-time work is **generally NOT permitted** — even with TOPIK, language students are not granted the work permit

💡 Money impact: at ₩10,000–12,000/hr ≈ $7–9/hr, a D-2 student can earn **$700–900/month** — enough to cover most living costs. A D-4 student can't.

### 🏥 #2: Health insurance timing
- **D-2**: NHIS from **alien registration date** (immediate, ~₩79,320/month ≈ $60)
- **D-4-1**: Insurance only kicks in **6 months after entry** — uncovered (or need private insurance) for first 6 months

### 🎓 #3: Academic pathway
- **D-2**: In the degree → diploma, admission scholarships, then switch to **D-10 (job-seeking)** → **E-7 (work visa)** after graduation
- **D-4**: Language only — no degree, no graduation, no direct job pathway; must apply to degree and switch to D-2 anyway

### 👨👩👧 #4: Family invitation
- **D-2 (degree)**: Can invite spouse/children (dependent visas)
- **D-4**: Generally cannot invite family

### ✅ When D-4 is still the right choice
1. **No TOPIK yet** → can't meet degree admission (e.g. Yonsei needs TOPIK 5), so language school on D-4 is the **bridging step**
2. **Want to try Korean first** before committing 4 years → D-4 is low commitment
3. **Rejected from degree / low GPA** → D-4 lets you stay and re-apply

**Key message: D-4 should be a temporary bridge, not the end goal. The moment the student qualifies, switch to D-2 (사증변경/status change) for work rights + insurance + family + graduation pathway.**

### 📌 One-line answer for students
> "If you can meet the admission requirements now, choose **D-2** — you can legally work, get health insurance immediately, and you're on the path to a degree and a work visa. **D-4** is only for language study before you qualify — use it as a stepping stone, then switch to D-2."

Sources: immigration.go.kr, Seoul Global Center, KoWork D-2/D-4 guide, NHIS/Ajou student notices, U&AN admin office (NHIS enrollment timing table).
